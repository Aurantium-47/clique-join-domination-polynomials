# Primary source and precise use

Bilal Ahmad Rather, *On domination polynomials of some graphs*, Journal of Combinatorial Mathematics and Combinatorial Computing **126** (2025), 279–289. [DOI 10.61091/jcmcc126-19](https://doi.org/10.61091/jcmcc126-19); [publisher PDF](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/volume%20126/on-domination-polynomials-of-some-graphs.pdf).

The fixed 11-page PDF is 960215 bytes, SHA-256 `1ea8f08566fa8029588683d5844e51970ba4e7c1cc950072039bc11181604166`. Printed page 286 was read as both text and a rendered PDF page. The graph family is introduced immediately before Theorem 2.16, with the central clique corresponding to the source's n_1 and the remaining clique orders corresponding to its other n_i.

Theorem 2.16's first two terms combine to `((1+x)^r-1)(1+x)^M`; its product term accounts for nonempty selections from every blade. The note proves that formula directly by a disjoint counting partition. The printed Corollary 2.17 has another `((1+x)^r-1)` after the already combined term. That display is not imported into the proof. This is an explicit choice of the definition/direct counting and Theorem 2.16, not a claim that the author has issued a correction.

The paragraph immediately after Theorem 2.16 poses an open question about zeros and their limits, log-concavity and unimodality. It is not an affirmative conjecture of universal log-concavity. The 17-vertex example has distinct blade orders; the equal-order infinite family is separately stated under the note's explicit graph definition.

This preparation used the retained primary source and existing proof review, without a new literature search. No global-priority or firstness certification is claimed. The third-party PDF and page image are not part of the distributed payload.
